About

Ali Modarresi

Seyed Alireza Alhosseini Almodarresieh — independent AI researcher and systems builder. The work connects AI safety, software provenance, biological computing, and traceable agent systems through a single question: how can intelligent systems become more inspectable?

What the work is

The work is a set of public, forkable artifacts — specifications, prototypes, and small experiments — that approach a single question from several angles: how can intelligent systems become more inspectable, trustworthy, and useful? The angles differ. EvoGuard approaches it through software provenance — gating AI-touched code on inspectable evidence. OrganoidOS approaches it through an open specification for a substrate where the usual inspectability assumptions break down. Neuro-Continuum and Hanna AI approach it through agent architectures that must remain traceable under real workloads. Onturgy and Kintsugi approach it through the underlying epistemics — what an architecture must make explicit, and how provisional a piece of reasoning really is.

How the projects connect

The projects are not a portfolio in the usual sense. They are a working graph: each one tests a hypothesis that one of the others needs but cannot justify on its own. EvoGuard needs a notion of provenance that Onturgy can describe but cannot itself enforce. OrganoidOS needs an evaluation discipline that Neuro-Continuum is exploring in a different domain. Kintsugi's fragility index is, in a loose sense, the same idea as EvoGuard's evidence chain — applied to clinical reasoning instead of code.

None of these connections is a theorem. They are working hypotheses about how the projects relate, and they are revisable.

What this page does and does not claim

  • Independent. The work is independent. No employer, funder, institution, or affiliation is claimed or implied.
  • No credentials claimed. No degrees, certifications, awards, peer-review status, or publication record are claimed on this page. Public profiles are linked; what they contain is for the reader to verify.
  • No citizenship, education, or employment inferred. None of those is mentioned, because none is needed to evaluate the work.
  • No inflated biography. The point of this page is to help a reader decide whether the work is worth reading. It is not a list of accomplishments.

How to read the work

The best entry point is the projects page. Pick the project whose problem interests you most, open its repository, and read its README before reading anything on this site. The repositories are the primary artifacts; this website is an index.

If you are considering collaboration, the research page explains how the directions relate, and the homepage's collaboration section lists the most reliable channels for getting in touch.

Crazy AI

Crazy AI is a secondary creative and research identity — a name under which ideas that sound unconventional at first are developed through open specifications, prototypes, and critical evaluation. It is not an incorporated company, a funded laboratory, or an established organization. It does not replace the name Ali Modarresi; it sits alongside it as a research persona.
